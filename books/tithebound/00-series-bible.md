# THE TITHEBOUND — Series Bible

*Working reference for every book in the series. If a chapter contradicts this file, fix one or the other before moving on.*

---

## 1. The pitch

**One line:** In a city where every spell costs years of someone's life and the rich buy those years from the poor, a girl whose lifespan secretly refills sells herself to a magic academy as a living battery — and is bought by the cold heir of the family hunting for exactly what she is.

**Genre:** Adult / new-adult dark academy romantasy.
**Romance:** Slow-burn enemies-to-lovers. One kiss in Book 1. Closed-door / fade-to-black throughout (can be raised later if the market asks for it). Guaranteed happy ending for the main couple by Book 5.
**Tone:** Sharp, funny narrator in a cruel world. Gothic atmosphere, high stakes, real grief, but never hopeless.
**Why readers binge it:** Every chapter ends on a hook. Every book solves its own mystery and opens a bigger one.

**Positioning (internal only — never put other authors or titles in the KDP listing):** For readers who love magic-school stories grown up, the "every spell has a cost" magic of hard fantasy, class-war dystopias, and slow-burn romantasy.

## 2. The world

### The city of Lanthorn
A walled river city built on the delta of the **Sallow**. Everything in Lanthorn smells faintly of candle fat: the city's main industry is the **Candleworks**. The candle imagery is deliberate and should run through the prose — people *burn*, *gutter*, *go out*.

- **The Gilt** — the upper city on the white terraces. Glass gardens, marble, the towers of the Seven Houses. People here live two and three hundred years.
- **The Wick** — the lower city on the river flats. Tenements, tallow, fog, broker shops. People here die around forty.
  - *Tallow Lane* — where Tam and Kit live (two rooms above a chandler's).
  - *Broker's Row* — where years are bought and sold.
  - *The Sump* — the poorest flats, flooded each spring.
  - *Saltmarket* — the next district over, where Tam sells years under false names.
- **The Hours Bridge** — a long stone bridge with twenty-four lamps linking the Wick bank to the Meridian's island.

### The Meridian
A college of black basalt on an island in the Sallow, where the heirs of the Gilt learn hourcraft.
- **The Horologe** — the great clock tower at the center. Its face is as wide as a pond. Its single hand has pointed at **eleven** for three hundred years. (Wick saying: *"The rich stopped the clock so they'd never have to go home."*) The hand moves for the first time at the end of Book 1.
- **The Tithe Hall** — the tithes' dormitory, half below the waterline. Damp, cold, always dripping.
- **The cisterns** — huge flooded vaults under the Tithe Hall. Off-limits.
- **The Undercroft** — sealed chambers beneath the Horologe. Officially empty. Not empty.
- **The Dial** — the circular duelling floor, laid out like a clock face.
- **The Library of Echoes** — the library, plus glass "echo-lenses" that can hold a recorded Echo.
- **The Refectory**, **the Gallery of Hours** (portraits of the Long-Lived), **the tower rooms** (where final-year wrights live; Severin's is directly beneath the stopped clockworks).

### Calendar and terms
- Week: six workdays plus **Seventhday**. Tithes' counts are **weighed** every Seventhday.
- Year: the Meridian term in Book 1 runs from **Tithe Day** (first day of autumn) through **the Long Night** (midwinter ball) to **the Reckoning** (end of winter — public final trial).

## 3. The magic: hourcraft

### The count
Everyone is born with a **count** — their remaining lifespan, shown as glowing gold marks along the inside of the left forearm, from wrist toward elbow.
- One long gold line per year, stacked from the wrist up toward the elbow like the rungs of a ladder. A short row of notches across the wrist bone for the months. A faint shimmer at the tip of the highest line for the days.
- Counts tick down naturally as you live, from the top. Spent time goes dark mark by mark — *it gutters*, browning at the ends first, then going grey.
- **Market fraud runs the other way:** Wick candidates paint on fake gold lines to look longer and fetch a bigger Grace, so Market binders tap the top line to prove it's real. Nobody in Lanthorn's history has ever *hidden* years at a Tithe Market.
- **"What's your count?"** is the Wick's real question. *"How old are you?"* is something only the Gilt bothers with.
- In the Gilt, showing your count is vulgar. Nobles wear jeweled **count-cuffs** over their forearms. In the Wick, your count is how you are priced.
- Official doctrine: counts are God-given, "the Allotment". (Truth, revealed in Book 3: the Horologe assigns them.)

### Spending time
**Hourcraft** is the art of spending time to work magic. A spell is a **working**. Practitioners are **hourwrights** (or **wrights**). Casting burns time from a count, and you can watch your marks go dark as you cast.

Core workings (keep the list short and visual):
| Working | Effect | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Stilling** | Freeze an object or person in time | Minutes for a cup; years for a room |
| **Quickening** | Speed a process forward (healing a wound means aging it closed) | Hours to months |
| **Unwinding** | Run an object backward (mend a shattered glass) | Days to years |
| **Hastening** | Move faster than the eye; duelling staple | Hours per second |
| **Echo** | Replay the recent past of a place, visibly, like ghosts | Weeks to months |
| **Reading** | A binder draws one minute to read a count exactly ("true reading") | One minute |

Workings shimmer gold. Large ones make the air ring like a struck glass.

### Moving time between people: tethers
- Time can only move between two people through a **tether**: a silver thread sewn under the skin around both wrists by a **binder**.
- The holder of a tether (the **draw**) can pull time from the other (the **well** — or, at the Meridian, the **tithe**) to fuel workings or simply add to their own count.
- A tethered pair can feel each other faintly: a second pulse, heat, strong emotions.
- Every legal tether is registered in **the Ledger**, the great registry kept by House Thane. Every legal transfer is recorded.
- **Black thread** — illegal tethers from Wick back-alley binders. Dangerous; kills one in three; fatal on children.
- **The Ledger never sells years to the Wick. It only buys.** Years flow uphill.
- Brokers in the Wick buy years for coin (cheaply) and sell them up to the Houses (expensively).

### Tallow ink
A black-market cosmetic sold by inksmiths like Mother Gannet. It darkens count-marks so they look spent. Wick people use it to hide high counts from brokers' thugs and debt collectors. It fools the eye, not a true reading. It must be reapplied daily, smudges with sweat and blood, and is expensive. **Tam's supply of ink is a running ticking clock in Book 1.**

### Wellsprings
Legend: the city's founders were **wellsprings**, people whose counts refilled endlessly. They were hunted, used, and finally "drowned" in the Sallow three hundred years ago in **the Drowning** — the same year the Horologe's hand stopped.
**Truth (series spine):** The founders didn't drown the wellsprings. They chained them under the Horologe and built the Ledger on their endless time. The clock is an engine that feeds on wellsprings and allots counts to the city. Maren Fenn is the last wellspring, and she is chained there now.

### The mother's tether (Tam's secret, fully explained)
Maren, a hidden wellspring, secretly tethered her daughter at birth with an old Wick folk-binding called a **mother's tether**. It holds the child's count where it stood the last time the binding was renewed. Maren renewed it the night she was taken, when Tam was nine and her count stood at **71 years, 4 months**. Ever since, any time Tam loses is pulled back through the tether from her mother's endless supply. Small losses refill in seconds; large ones over hours ("fast for a cup, slow for a bucket").
**Tam doesn't know any of this until the end of Book 1.** She thinks she is a freak, or cursed, or a wellspring.

## 4. Society

- **The Seven Houses** (the Ledger Houses): **Thane** (keeps the Ledger; colors black and silver), **Crane** (gold), **Penhallow** (plum), **Vey** (green), **Ostrander** (blue), **Marchbank** (rust), **Lisle** (white).
- **The Long-Lived** — nobles who have bought their way past a hundred. They sit in the Gallery during public trials and are treated like living saints.
- **Greycoats** — Wardens of the Ledger, the city's police, in grey coats with silver buttons.
- **Binders** — licensed Ledger tether-makers, also in Ledger grey.
- **The Tithe** — every autumn the Meridian buys tithes at the **Tithe Market**: Wick youths aged 18 to 21 who serve their wright for the whole term as a living reserve of time.
  - Houses bid in **years of Grace**: years paid from the House's reserves to a family member the tithe names. Normal Grace is 5 to 10 years. **Tam's 30 years is unheard of.**
  - Tithes are legally House property for the term. If a tithe's count falls below ten years, they're "sent home short" — if they can walk.
  - Tithes wear grey. Wrights wear black with a sash in their House color.
- **The Unwound** — a whispered Wick resistance that cuts black threads, hides runaways and smuggles tithes out of the city. Magister Vadra is one of them.

### Slang and texture (Wick speech)
- *long / short* — high / low count. ("He's long. She's short.")
- *burning* — spending time. *Gone grey* — dead. *Thread* — a tether.
- *Count's honest* — someone is trustworthy. *Inked* — lying.
- Oaths: *"Saints' teeth."* *"Hells."* *"Burn me."*
- Gilt speech is formal and elaborate; nobles talk about years the way connoisseurs talk about wine.

## 5. Characters

### Tamsin "Tam" Fenn — POV narrator (19)
- **Count:** 71 years, 4 months. **Frozen since she was nine.** Shown as 61 and falling under her ink.
- **Look:** Wick-thin, dark hair she cuts herself, ink-stained fingers, a scar through one eyebrow (Bry, age seven).
- **Background:** Worked for Mother Gannet as a *counter*, the person who verifies counts in time sales. Reads counts better than most binders. Knows ink. Has sold years under false names in Saltmarket and got them back by morning, which is how she and Kit have survived.
- **Want:** Get Kit his Grace and get through the term without anyone seeing what her count does.
- **Need:** To learn she is worth more than what can be taken from her, and to stop carrying everything alone.
- **Wound:** Her mother "went into the river" ten years ago. Tam raised newborn Kit by herself from age nine.
- **Voice:** Wry, quick, practical, darkly funny. Notices prices, exits and lies. Underneath, terrified of being loved for what she can give.

### Christopher "Kit" Fenn (10)
- **Count:** Born with 11 years (sickly lungs). **One year left** at the start of Book 1. After the 30-year Grace: 31 years.
- Draws maps of places he has never seen, built from his mother's stories as Tam retold them. **His Meridian map marks a spot under the clock: "where the clock keeps its heart."** (Plant in Ch. 1, pay off in Ch. 15 and the epilogue.)
- Believes the old wellspring tales. Asks too many questions. Adores Severin on sight.

### Severin Thane (21) — love interest
- Heir to House Thane, final-year wright, the most gifted caster at the Meridian.
- **Count:** Publicly unknown, always cuffed. **True count at the start of Book 1: 9 years.** He has burned some forty years of his own life casting so he never has to draw on a tithe.
  - After the Long Night: 7 years (he burns 5, Tam pushes 3 back). After Aurelia punishes him in Ch. 29: **4 years** going into Book 2.
- **Look:** Tall, all in black buttoned to the throat, black gloves that never come off, silver Thane cuff, eyes the exact grey of tallow ink.
- **Rumor:** "The Thane heir's tithes go home in boxes."
- **Truth:**
  - **Liesl** — at sixteen his grandmother made him draw on a Wick girl until she died, to "harden" him. He watched her count go out "like a row of candles."
  - **Orrin Fisk** — his tithe last year. Severin faked Orrin's death and smuggled him south. Orrin is alive (revealed in Ch. 25; an ally in Book 2).
- **Habit:** Repairs watches and clocks by hand, without magic, in a room full of ticking clocks. It's how he refuses to spend time.
- **Want:** Outlive his grandmother long enough to take the House and tear down the Ledger.
- **Need:** To stop dying in secret and let someone help.
- **Voice:** Clipped, precise, dry. Says less than he means. Cruel in public on purpose and kind in private by accident.
- **The deal:** He needs Tam's count to *look* drained whenever he casts, so his grandmother thinks he's drawing on her. An ink-cheat is the perfect partner.

### Hollis Penhallow (19) — best friend
- Novice wright of House Penhallow, an ancient house that sold its own years generations ago to pay its debts: the poor relations of the Gilt.
- **Count: 6 years, 2 months.** He jokes about it constantly.
- Flamboyant, kind, theatrical, brave in a way he pretends isn't bravery. Wears plum. Refuses to draw on his tithe Pell, so he's failing every practical.
- Gets the best lines. Readers must love him by Ch. 6.
- **Heroic beat, Book 1:** spends a month of his six years casting the Echo that exposes Lysander (Ch. 26).
- **Fate:** The series will be tempted to kill him. **Don't.** His survival is the payoff of Book 5's unbinding.

### Bryony "Bry" Kettle (20) — friend from the Wick
- Tithe to Lysander Crane. **Count: 58 years** at the Market → 31 by Ch. 16 → drained to two weeks in Ch. 23 → about 20 years after Tam saves her.
- Loud, fearless, bare-knuckle boxer, broke Tam's nose when they were seven and set it herself when they were eight. Hates the Gilt with her whole chest.

### Lysander Crane (20) — rival and Book 1 villain
- Novice wright, Severin's cousin (their mothers were sisters). Golden, charming in public, sadistic in private.
- Drains tithes for Aurelia in exchange for the promise of the Thane heirship.
- **Dies in Ch. 28,** drained in an instant by Aurelia so the murders end with him.

### Lady Aurelia Thane (247, looks 30) — series villain
- Keeper of the Ledger, Chancellor of the Meridian, Severin's great-great-great-grandmother. Everyone calls her "Grandmother."
- Warm voice, perfect manners, never hurries. Always eating something sweet: **candied violets** from a silver box.
- Has fed on Maren for ten years. But ten years ago **half of Maren's yield went missing.** She has been draining high-count tithes ever since, testing them to find the one who refills: the leak.
- Talks about years like vintages. *"Wick years are thin, but they're so plentiful."*

### Magister Ines Vadra (50s) — ambiguous mentor
- Professor of Tethering. Wick-born former tithe who survived her term, married into House Vey, and is the only Wick-born magister.
- Always wears long sleeves. Secretly a leader of the Unwound.
- Knows ink when she sees it. Helps Tam in cryptic ways. Has her own agenda: she wants a wellspring as a weapon against the Ledger.

### Maren Fenn — Tam's mother
- Believed drowned ten years ago. Actually a **wellspring**, chained in silver thread in the Undercroft beneath the Horologe's pendulum, drained daily by Aurelia.
- Taken a few weeks after Kit was born, before she could tether him. ("I was coming back for him.")
- **Book 1 final line of dialogue:** *"Tamsin. I told you never to come here."*

### Supporting cast
- **Steward Agathe Rook** — keeper of the Tithe Hall. Former tithe, gruff, fair, one milky eye. Weighs counts every Seventhday.
- **Mother Gannet** — Wick broker and inksmith, Tam's old employer. Sells ink and gossip. Fond of Tam in her own mercenary way.
- **Joss Mallory** — tithe to House Ostrander, count 66. Cheerful, talks about his girl Hetty back home. **First victim (Ch. 4–5).**
- **Anni Rusk** — tithe to House Lisle, count 63, from the Sump. Shy, sings under her breath. **Second victim (Ch. 13).**
- **Pell Darrow** — Hollis's tithe, count 49. A gentle giant who bakes. Utterly loyal to Hollis.
- **Captain Hesketh Marr** — Greycoat captain. Smart, tired, owned by the Houses but not entirely.
- **Clerk Aldous Penn** — the Ledger clerk who runs the Tithe Market.
- **Orrin Fisk** — Severin's former tithe, secretly alive in the south.

## 6. Series arc (5 books)

| # | Title | Core story | Ending hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A Tithe of Hours** | Tam is bought by Severin; tithes are being drained; Tam finds out who is hunting, and why. | Aurelia finds Tam. Maren is alive beneath the clock. The clock's hand moves. |
| 2 | **A Debt of Years** | Tam is tethered to Aurelia as her new well while Severin (4 years left) joins the Unwound in the Wick with Kit, Vadra and Orrin. Rescue of Maren from the inside. | Maren is freed, and the Horologe starts counting *down*. Every count in the Gilt begins to shrink. |
| 3 | **A Vault of Seconds** | The truth of the Drowning: the clock is an engine built on chained wellsprings, and it assigns everyone's count. Breaking it would return everyone to a natural lifespan and kill the Long-Lived. The city splits. | Maren dies stabilizing the clock. Tam inherits her mother's nature: she becomes a true wellspring. |
| 4 | **A Reckoning of Hearts** | War between the Gilt and the Wick. Severin takes House Thane by force. Tam is the most valuable person alive, and everyone wants her. The romance is tested by power. | Severin seemingly gives Tam up to Aurelia's allies to end the war. Darkest moment. |
| 5 | **An Hour Unbound** | Final assault on the Horologe. Tam and Severin unbind the clock together. | Counts return to natural life for everyone. Severin's burned years are restored. Hollis lives. HEA. |

**Romance arc across the series:** enemies with leverage (B1 start) → reluctant partners → in love (B1 end) → separated by captivity (B2) → together as equals (B3) → tested by power and betrayal (B4) → earned happy ending (B5).

## 7. Style guide (for every chapter)

1. **First person, past tense, Tam's voice.** (Book 1's epilogue is Severin's POV. Later books may alternate.)
2. **Chapters run 2,800–3,800 words.** Start in scene, never in summary.
3. **Every chapter ends on a hook:** a reveal, a threat, a question, or an emotional turn. No chapter ends with someone going to sleep.
4. **Show the world through conflict.** No lectures. If the world needs explaining, someone has to want something during the explanation.
5. **Counts on the page.** Whenever magic is cast, show the gold marks going dark. Readers should feel time as a physical cost.
6. **Candle imagery** (burn, gutter, wick, tallow, smoke) and **clock imagery** (ticking, hands, gears, stopping) are the series' two motifs. Use them, don't drown in them.
7. **Humor every chapter.** Tam's wit and Hollis's theatrics are the release valve.
8. **Romance: tension over touch.** Proximity, the tether's shared pulse, gloves, wrists. In Book 1 the first touch of skin to skin matters more than the kiss.
9. **Specific over abstract.** "A loaf, a pair of boots, and three weeks' rent," not "enough to survive."
10. **Banned phrasing (reads as machine-written):** "a testament to", "tapestry", "the weight of [noun]", "palpable", "unspoken", "in that moment", "a breath I didn't know I was holding", "electric" (for touch), "orbs", "something shifted", "let out a breath", "the air was thick with". Go easy on em-dashes. No more than one "I realized" per chapter.

## 9. Book 1 as written: facts Book 2 must honor

These came out of drafting and override anything above that disagrees.

**Calendar.** Months of the Meridian year: Harvestmonth, Leaffall, Mistmonth, Frostmonth (ends with the Long Night at midwinter), Deepwinter, Thaw. The Reckoning falls about three weeks after the Long Night. Wick people still say "February" for late winter.

**Geography.** The Gilt Road runs from the Hours Bridge straight up through the Wick to the terraces. The Thane passage: a hidden door in the tower stair, a spiral down, a Founders' brick tunnel under the Sallow to a derelict chandlery cellar near Broker's Row (Gannet's people use it). Halfway along, an old arch with an iron gate and chain leads down to the Undercroft; Severin could not break it. The cisterns are reached through the barred door at the end of the Tithe Hall (73 steps); across them, an iron door worked with clock faces opens onto the Undercroft passage, the small square room with wall rings, and a deeper passage carved with clock faces ending at the solid silver door. The Keeper's own stair (red carpet, clock-hand sconces) runs from a hidden door at the base of the Horologe to the same passage. Beyond the silver door: the round chamber, the pendulum, Maren.

**How Book 1 ends.**
- Tam is threaded to Aurelia with a Ledger thread. She also carries Severin's thread and the black thread to Bry (never cut). Her mother's tether is invisible.
- Maren can speak to Tam inside her head through the mother's tether. Her last words: "Wait for him."
- The Horologe's hand moved to one minute past eleven; the pendulum swung once. Every bell in Lanthorn rang.
- Liesl's watch, stopped at 4:20 for five years, started ticking at that moment. Kit's tin watch stopped for exactly one minute and then restarted.
- Lysander is dead, drained by Aurelia on the Dial. Aurelia publicly framed him for the murders and claimed Tam under the Founders' law ("Never again the well"). The Gilt cheered her.
- Severin (4y 2m) is with the Unwound at Gannet's, with Vadra, Hollis, Bry, Pell, and Kit, who has drawn the way to the chamber. Captain Marr quietly let him go and pointed him there.

**Lore established.**
- Wells run in families, mothers to daughters ("a well only shows when something is taken from it"). Kit may be one; nobody has tested it.
- The previous chained well lasted 140 years and died eleven years before Book 1. Aurelia found Maren the next year through a broker who saw her sell the same three years twice. Maren sold where she shouldn't have, knew they were coming, and tethered Tam the night she left.
- Silver threads run from Maren up into the clockworks and sideways through the walls (where to: Book 2).
- Broker Seven = the Ledger buying for itself. Statute 91 (the right of Echo) exists.
- Echoes are silent. A blank echo-lens can hold one and replay it, large, if a caster pays.
- A wright holding someone's hand on a thread can draw *through* them. A tithe can push time *up* a thread to a wright (Tam is the first known to do it).

**Characters added while drafting.** Wat Sorrel (Bry's cousin, copy-boy in the Ledger annex). Fitch Ostrander (Joss's gentle wright). Hetty Brannock (Joss's girl, Saltmarket). Mrs. Achterberg (across the landing on Tallow Lane). Tobias Wren (chandler below the Fenns). Pruett (broker, Broker's Row). Corrie (Vey tithe). Aurel Vey (Vadra's late husband, her wright). Celandine Penhallow (Hollis's aunt, died Year 275; Tam wore her dress). Orrin Fisk lives in Calder Bay with Marta and baby Wren. Severin stilled his heart for a day and a half to fake his death (cost: four years). Lord Featherstonehaugh (Hollis's stuffed owl).

**Allies now in place.** Steward Rook (knows about Orrin and covers for them). Captain Marr ("I'm sorry, counter"). Magister Vadra and Mother Gannet (Unwound). Severin's man from the room over the pie shop.

## 10. Series arc, revised before Book 2

The Book 2 outline (`book-2-a-debt-of-years/outline.md`) sets the clock's full rules: the Stay, the Keeper's Knot, the Renewal, the Lines, slips, thread-speech, and the Settlement at midnight. The later books now run like this:

- **Book 2, A Debt of Years:** Maren is freed. Lotte Varga dies cutting the Stay. The hand walks a minute a day toward twelve and debts of years drain home. Last line: Kit's count is getting shorter, because his Grace is *given* years.
- **Book 3, A Vault of Seconds:** the 54-day countdown. Midnight would save the Wick and Hollis and give Bry back her stolen years. It would kill the Long-Lived, Rook and Vadra, take Kit's Grace, and take back every year Tam ever gave Severin. The city and the friends split over whether to let it strike. Aurelia and Ismay Lisle want a new well chained under the clock to stop it, and they expect Tam to volunteer for the people she loves. Ending as planned: Maren dies stabilizing the clock in her daughter's place, and Tam's finger crooks as she wakes as a true well.
- **Books 4 and 5** stand as planned: the war, Severin taking House Thane, the apparent betrayal, and the final unbinding, where counts return to natural lives for everyone, Severin's burned years are restored, and Hollis lives.
